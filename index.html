What this does

Say you own a Citadel paint but want a similar color from another brand, maybe because it is cheaper or you already own it. Pick the Citadel paint and this page lists paints from other brands that look about the same.

How to use it

  1. Pick your Citadel paint from the dropdown.
  2. Look at the big color block. That is the color you want.
  3. Compare it with the small swatches below. Closer to the big block means a better match.
  4. Note the brand and paint name, then look for that paint in a shop.

What the labels mean

  • Very close: most people will not see a difference once it is painted.
  • Close: clearly the same color, but a little lighter, darker, or warmer.
  • Rough match: same color family, but you will notice a difference.
  • Paints that are too different are hidden on purpose.

If you see "No close matches," this page just does not have a good one in its list yet. It does not mean no paint exists.

CIELAB or RGB?

RGB compares colors the way a screen makes them, as a mix of red, green, and blue light. It is simple, but it can pick matches that look a bit odd to your eyes.

CIELAB measures color closer to how people actually see it, so its matches usually look better. Leave it on CIELAB, and switch to RGB if you are curious how the results change.

Paint words worth knowing

  • Base / layer paints: the normal, opaque paints. This page is meant for these.
  • Metallic paints: contain tiny metal flakes and sparkle. Match metallics only with metallics. This page does that for you: a metallic Citadel paint is only compared with other metallics.
  • Washes, shades, and contrast paints: thin, runny paints that settle into crevices. Their final color depends on the color underneath, so a screen swatch is not reliable. Do not use this page for them.

Tips before you buy

  • Test the new paint on a spare model or a piece of plastic first.
  • Paint dries a little darker or lighter than it looks wet.
  • Apply two thin coats rather than one thick one, so details stay sharp.
  • Screen colors vary by device and lighting. Treat this as a good starting guess, not a guarantee.

The Two Thin Coats box

Two Thin Coats paints come in sets of three tones: shadow (darkest), midtone, and highlight (lightest). The company publishes its own chart of which Citadel paint to use instead of each one. When your Citadel paint is on that chart, this page shows the Two Thin Coats paint it lines up with, and the chart's Army Painter pick too. These come from the paint maker, not from color math, so they have no color swatch.

Hiding brands you don't use

Tap a brand's name, or the Two Thin Coats heading, to collapse or expand it. The page remembers your choice on this device, even when you pick a different paint or come back later.

Spotted a wrong color?

Press "Report a problem" under any paint. It opens a short form on GitHub with the paint details already filled in. Add what looks wrong and send it. You need a free GitHub account, and reports are public, so don't include personal details.
